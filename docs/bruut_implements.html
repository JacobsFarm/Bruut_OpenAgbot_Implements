Implement · Sowing & Planting

Overseeder

Grassland slit seeder. 1 m modules with 8 rows at 125 mm and coupling flanges. A thin disc cuts a 15 mm slot, the seed is placed about 12 mm deep and a sprung press wheel closes the slot. Every row follows the ground on its own arm; two gas springs push the floating lift frame down with robot weight.

2 variants: Simple · Advanced
Folder: overseeder/

Overseeder · variant 1 of 2

Simple

Angled single disc (7°, Ø300 × 3) with a seed boot in its lee and a depth ring. Gravity-fed seed hopper with a compartment for grass (41 l) and one for fine seed (18 l), each with its own cam roller and a motor that follows the driving speed. Built on the headstock and lift frame of the simple applicator v2.

  • 8 rows at 125 mm = 1 m per module
  • Draft force approx. 335 N (normal)
  • Depth within ±3 mm: 94 %
  • About €1,675 in parts

Folder: overseeder/simple/

Overseeder · variant 2 of 2

Advanced

Parallel linkage so the toolbar stays parallel, straight Ø300 × 3 disc with depth rings on both sides, a curved seed coulter directly behind the disc and a press wheel in a fork. Air seeder (62 + 17 l hopper, 12 V fan) on a frame above the rear axle blows the seed through 8 hoses to the coulters.

  • 8 rows at 125 mm = 1 m per module
  • 2 gas springs in the slotted hole
  • Depth within ±3 mm: 98 %
  • About €2,280 in parts

Power harrow / rotary tiller

Not worked out yet

What it does

Driven tines or blades work the top soil into a seedbed, or loosen the soil between vines and trees. Seen on vineyard and orchard robots (Slopehelper power harrow, Pellenc RX-20, Naïo Ted) and small electric tractors.

Concept (first thoughts)

A narrow rear-mounted rotor with vertical tines (power harrow) or L-blades (rotary tiller) and a crumbler roller that sets the depth, driven by a geared electric motor. Lifted by a linear actuator like the grassland harrow. Works in strips, so the power stays within what the robot can supply.

Reuse from existing implements

Rear mount and lift of the grassland harrow; gearmotor and chain drive of the dockweed drill; power supply from the battery pack or hybrid range extender.

Open questions
  • Working width versus available power and traction
  • Rotor speed, tine type and working depth
  • Draft and reaction forces on a light robot
  • Stones: overload protection of the rotor

A module that straddles one ridge, with a camera or a sensor that detects spear tips breaking the plastic or soil, a narrow cutting and gripping tool on a vertical axis that goes into the ridge, and a crate on the robot. The robot creeps along the ridge.

Reuse from existing implements

Vertical axis and drill head layout of the dockweed drill; camera hood of the potato disease scout; crate carrier deck for the harvested spears.

Open questions
  • Detecting spears before they reach the light
  • Cut depth and closing the ridge again
  • Cycle time per spear versus the robot's speed
  • Working under the plastic film
