# Insect vacuum (simple): rationale

## Push-pull instead of only suction

A suction slot only pulls air near its mouth: by the Dallavalle formula the speed in front of a 25 mm slot drops from
11 m/s in the slot to 1.5 m/s at 50 mm and 0.8 m/s at 100 mm. That does not lift a lygus bug (terminal velocity
~3.5 m/s) from under a leaf. A blowing jet keeps its speed much longer (a plane jet decays only with the square root
of the distance): the 8 mm knives give ~23 m/s at the near row and ~13 m/s at the far row. So the knives do the work
of dislodging the insects and the nozzles only have to collect the air that the knives blow in. Because the hood is
closed (brush skirt), the knife air has nowhere to go but into the nozzles: the flows balance.

Blowing from low at the sides and upward follows the way lygus bugs and many other pests hide: under the leaves and in
the flowers. The 30° angle lifts them toward the nozzles instead of pressing them into the soil.

## One fan, closed loop

The fan sucks the clean air out of the catch box (after the mesh) and blows it back to the knives. One fan does both
jobs, the impeller never sees an insect (the basket keeps them for counting: a pest-pressure map per pass), and no
air is blown out over the field, which would scatter insects to the neighbouring bed.

## Reusing the drop-on-demand hood

The boom's hood (x ±300, y ±262) is already checked between the tyres and against the 30° steering sweep, is dark
(no light needed here, but no wind either) and hangs at a known height over a bed with 2 rows at ±150. The insect
vacuum only adds holes for the 3 stubs in the top sheet and the parts inside; the boom's camera gland and feed-hose
hole are plugged.

## Catch box on the upper beams

The suction hoses go straight up through the open middle of the frame; there are no bends to collect insects. The box
stands on 2 rails on 2 cross tubes at y ±325 (the upper beams have grid holes only at |y| 275 … 625), clear of the
wheel-unit frame bolts at y ±425 / ±575. The mesh basket lifts out at the end of a bed; the insects can be frozen and
counted, or tipped into a bucket.

## Beetles

Colorado beetles and larvae are 15-20 times heavier than lygus bugs. The jet lifts them only at the near row; they
also let go and drop when the plant is shaken. For potatoes a variant with beaters that knock them into a tray under
the canopy (the second concept in the idea) would catch more; this simple variant aims at the light pests of soft
fruit.

## Beneficials

The same air lifts ladybirds and parasitic wasps. The controller can lower the fan speed in flowering beds, and the
basket count (pest vs beneficial) gives the farmer the data to decide how often to run it.
